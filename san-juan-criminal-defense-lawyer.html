Grand Jury Questions Answered

A grand jury functions as a charging body, reviewing information presented by the government and deciding whether to issue an indictment under the governing standard. Its sessions are generally private, and the rules for presenting testimony, documents, and legal arguments may differ from those used in a public trial. San Juan Criminal Defense Lawyer can explain the local framework and help a person understand how the inquiry may affect witnesses, targets, or organizations. When records or testimony are requested, San Juan Criminal Defense Lawyer may help evaluate confidentiality concerns and the practical steps for responding.

Someone asked to testify should separate firsthand recollection from assumptions and should not guess when an answer is unknown. The privilege against self-incrimination may protect some responses, but its scope, timing, and exceptions depend on the circumstances and applicable law. San Juan Criminal Defense Lawyer can discuss whether counsel may be present under local rules, when a witness may leave to seek advice, and whether immunity or another protection should be considered. If a question is unclear or calls for information outside the witness's knowledge, San Juan Criminal Defense Lawyer may recommend seeking clarification before answering.

Grand jury secrecy can limit what prosecutors, jurors, witnesses, and other participants may disclose, although the boundaries vary by jurisdiction and by the person receiving the information. Avoid discussing the investigation with potential witnesses or posting about it online, and route questions from outside parties through an appropriate channel. San Juan Criminal Defense Lawyer can help assess what happens after testimony, including whether further contact is expected and how confidentiality obligations apply. Timely, jurisdiction-specific guidance can reduce misunderstandings while an investigation is active.
